The decision the notes are really teaching

Can I get both sides to one base? Yes → equate indices. No → take logs.

16x−1=64x16^{x-1}=6^{4x}16x−1=64x looks like a log question, but the notes reduce it first:

24x−4=24x×34x  ⇒  2−4=34x2^{4x-4}=2^{4x}\times 3^{4x} \;\Rightarrow\; 2^{-4}=3^{4x}24x−4=24x×34x⇒2−4=34x

Method fingerprint

  • Common base first, always — 9→3², 16 and 6→2 and 3, 8→2³, 27→3³, 64 and 16→4³.
  • Constant factors split off the index: 2^(4x+1) → 2^(4x)×2.
  • Once bases match, equate indices to get a linear equation.
  • Disguised quadratics by explicit substitution: 'Let 3ˣ be y'.
  • Logs taken only when bases cannot be reconciled.
  • Answers may be left in terms of log 2 and log 3.
