One base, or logarithms

Can I get both sides to one base?

This one question decides every exponent problem. Answer it before you write anything: logarithms are the last resort, not the first move. Even 16x−1 = 64x, which looks like a log question, gets reduced first.

Each press takes one more shape down the fork.

Get to a common base first, every time. These are the ones that keep coming up:

9x+1 + 1 = 10 × 3x

A quadratic in disguise: a term in 32x sitting next to a term in 3x. Name the repeated thing and the costume comes off.

The two roots in y are two values of 3x, and each gives one x.

9x+1 + 1 = 10 × 3x
32x+2 + 1 = 10 × 3x
32x × 3² + 1 = 10 × 3x
Let 3x be y.
⊕ extra working

A value of y is not an answer — put it back into 3x = y and read off the x.

9y² − 10y + 1 = 0
(9y − 1)(y − 1) = 0
y = 1/9 or y = 1
3x = 3−2, so x = −2   3x = 30, so x = 0

16x−1 = 64x, in terms of log 2 and log 3

16 is a power of 2 and 6 is not, so the bases cannot be reconciled. Push the index laws as far as they go first — that cancels a whole factor — and only then take logs. Taking the log of each side turns both curves into straight lines, and the answer is where they cross.

Hover a line to light up the side it came from.

16x−1 = 64x
24x−4 = 24x × 34x
2−4 = 34x
log(2−4) = log(34x)
−4 log 2 = 4x log 3
x = −4 log 2 ÷ (4 log 3)
= −log 2 ÷ log 3
⊕ extra working

That answer is exact as it stands; the decimal is only here so the picture can be drawn.

23x × 8y = ¼  and  27x ÷ 3y+2 = ⅑

Each equation reduces on its own — one in base 2, one in base 3 — and the exponentials vanish completely. What is left is two straight lines.

Hover a line to light up the equation it came from.

23x × 8y = ¼  and  27x ÷ 3y+2 = ⅑
23x × 23y = 2−2
3x + 3y = −2
33x ÷ 3y+2 = 3−2
3x − y − 2 = −2
⊕ extra working

Two straight lines meet in one place, and that place is the answer.

3x − y = 0, so y = 3x
3x + 9x = −2, so x = −1/6 and y = −1/2
