Reading, splitting and solving

log₄32

A logarithm asks one question: what power do I raise this base to, to get this number? So the opening line is always the same — rewrite it as a power.

4 and 32 both sit on the line of powers of 2, which is what makes the answer exact.

log₄32 = x
4x = 32
22x = 25
2x = 5
x = 5/2

⊕ extra working

Every logarithm here has to be to the same base and x and y have to be positive, or the separate terms do not exist — and a sum inside a logarithm cannot be split at all.

log₂x + log₄x = 3

Two logarithms of the same number to different bases are not like terms. Changing base is a rescaling: log₄x is exactly half of log₂x at every x, because 4 = 2². Once both are in base 2 they add, and the sum meets y = 3 in one place.

Drag x at the top and the two readings hold a ratio of 2 to 1.

log₄x = log₂x ÷ log₂4 = ½ log₂x
log₂x + ½ log₂x = (3/2) log₂x = 3
log₂x = 2, so x = 4
Check: log₂4 + log₄4 = 2 + 1 = 3

Substituting, and which roots to throw away

One substitution unlocks both of these. The difference is what the substituted thing is allowed to be: a logarithm may be negative, an exponential may not.

Both curves cross zero at u = −1, and only one of them may use it.

(log₂x)² − log₂x³ − 4 = 0, let u = log₂x, so log₂x³ = 3u
u² − 3u − 4 = 0, so (u − 4)(u + 1) = 0
u = 4 gives x = 2⁴ = 16  keep
u = −1 gives x = 2−1 = 1/2  keep, a logarithm may be negative
